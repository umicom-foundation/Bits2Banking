# Describe a part in three ways

[Course contents](index.md) · [Previous lesson — Count, group and keep track of units](01-count-in-cents.md) · [Next lesson — Follow increases, decreases and grouping](03-signed-numbers-and-order.md)

## What you will learn

Convert between a fraction, a decimal and a percentage, then calculate a part of an amount.

## Read a fraction

A **fraction** writes how many equal shares you chose over how many shares there are in the whole. In 3/4, the bottom 4 means four equal shares and the top 3 means take three. A **decimal** uses place value after a point. A **percentage** counts parts out of 100.

## Work it through

1. Mark four equal sections in one paper strip and shade three. Each section is 1/4 of the strip.
2. Divide 3 by 4 to get 0.75. Seventy-five hundredths is 75/100, so 3/4 = 0.75 = 75%.
3. To find 15% of an 800-cent workshop fee, read 15% as 15 out of 100. Calculate 800 × 15 ÷ 100 = 120 cents.
4. A discount of 120 cents leaves 800 − 120 = 680 cents. Here, 800 cents is the starting value. Applying a second discount to 680 cents would use a different starting value.

## Try it and check

Write 1/5 as a decimal and a percentage. Then find 20% of 350 cents and subtract that amount from 350 cents.

**Check:** 1/5 = 0.2 = 20%. The reduction is 70 cents and the new total is 280 cents. Divide the reduction by the original 350 cents if you want to check the reduction is 0.2 of the whole.

## Apply it to an Umicom project

Write an Umicom Bank practice display with fields for starting cents, discount percent and final cents. Label the discount percent’s starting value, and use whole-cent practice answers here. A real fee feature would need explicit rounding rules and separate verification.
