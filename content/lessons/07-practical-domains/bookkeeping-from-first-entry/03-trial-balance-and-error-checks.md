# Prepare a trial balance and find its limits

[Course contents](index.md) · [Previous lesson — Record both sides of a transaction](02-double-entry-journal-and-ledger.md) · [Next lesson — Reconcile the cash book with a statement](04-reconcile-the-cash-records.md)

## What you will learn

Prepare a trial balance from ledger balances, and explain one error that balanced columns cannot reveal.

## Add the ledger balances

A **trial balance** lists account balances by debit and credit side on a specific date. It checks that the posted ledger still balances. It uses the resulting account balances, after the debits and credits for each individual account have been combined.

## Work it through

Use the ledger after the 500-cent loan repayment.

| Account | Debit balance in cents | Credit balance in cents |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Cash | 10000 | 0 |
| Equipment | 2500 | 0 |
| Loan Payable | 0 | 2500 |
| Owner Capital | 0 | 10000 |
| **Total** | **12500** | **12500** |

1. List the accounts once and mark the date. For each account enter its balance on the correct side.
2. Total each column. Equality gives one useful check.
3. Imagine the equipment receipt was entered under Supplies instead. The debit total would be the same. Compare the account name with the source document to find this kind of mistake.
4. Imagine the whole equipment purchase was omitted. Both columns would still balance. Compare the journal references with the source document list as well.

## Try it and check

Suppose Equipment shows 2050 cents debit but the other balances are unchanged. Find the difference and sketch the evidence you would inspect.

**Check:** The debit column is 12050 cents and the credit column is 12500 cents. The difference is 450 cents. Compare the Equipment ledger with E-01 and the related Cash credit. Correct a wrong figure through the reviewed journal workflow. A number added solely to equalise both columns hides the error.

## Apply it to an Umicom project

Draw two checks for an Umicom Bank practice report. One compares total debits and credits, and one compares journal references and original receipts. Give each check a clear pass or mismatch result so a single balanced total cannot hide a missing transaction.
