# Teaching notes — Treasury Stage 1

This is a **reader-first course**. Begin by reading the story rather than displaying a software architecture. Ask one question at a time. Do not assume the audience knows what a bank balance, currency, trade or settlement is.

The community kitchen example is carried across the first four chapters so learners can reuse knowledge instead of repeatedly meeting unrelated jargon. Chapters 5–12 introduce a fictional company and bank dealing in currencies. All amounts are invented; they are not market quotes or recommended financial transactions.

When a learner struggles with an answer, return to the transaction. Ask: **Who owes what? In which currency? On what date? Has it merely been planned, approved or actually completed?** These questions are often more useful than adding another technical definition.

Do not require Calypso, vendor licences, Java, C23 or PowerShell for Stage 1. More advanced material and the practical native lab are optional extensions. Children should use fictional transactions and non-privileged devices with adult supervision where appropriate.

The original Umicom books and presentation contain detailed diagrams; acknowledge those sources. Never describe a historical Calypso code example as a verified API for an unspecified current release. For professional audiences, contrast the simplified teaching lifecycle with their licensed product configuration.
