# Service through knowledge and technology

**Approximate running time:** 5–7 minutes

## Introduction

When people talk about technology, they often begin with computers, programming languages or the latest devices. Those are important tools. But a useful question comes before all of them: what do we hope to achieve with the skills we learn?

For Umicom, learning technology is connected to a broader purpose. The Umicom Foundation is guided by values of service, mutual respect, fairness and sharing knowledge. Within the Islamic tradition, learning and helping others are not separate from the way we conduct our daily lives. They form part of our responsibilities towards the people around us.

## Knowledge becomes useful through action

Imagine you have learned to write a small computer programme that keeps track of notes. It is a modest achievement, but it teaches you how to save information and find it again. You can use that knowledge to help a student organise lessons, a volunteer coordinate appointments or a group maintain a task list.

As you learn more, your skills can have a wider impact. You may be able to add permissions so that people see only their own information. You may be able to create an audit trail so changes can be explained, or write a test that protects an important calculation from future errors.

These improvements are not just technical details. They can make software more dependable for the people using it.

## Trust and financial systems

Consider a simple example. A small organisation receives a donation of fifty pounds. A reliable accounting system records the receipt and the corresponding income. When twelve pounds are spent on supplies, it records that payment too.

If the software loses an entry, displays a misleading balance or allows an unauthorised payment, trust is damaged. That is why developers learn about balanced journals, validation, permissions and reconciliation. Each of those concepts is a way to make the system easier to check and harder to misuse.

The full worked example is in the double-entry accounting lesson on the Bits to Banking website.

## Learning together

You do not need to be an experienced developer to help. You can begin by correcting an unclear sentence in a lesson, testing a set of instructions or explaining a new concept to someone who is starting out.

As you become more confident, you can explore the Umicom Framework source code, study how its modules are connected and contribute a small improvement. When you document your work carefully, the next learner begins with more understanding than you had when you started.

We Value Each Other and Together Everyone Achieves More.

Service to Many Leads to Greatness.

## Conclusion

Learning is a journey, and a useful piece of knowledge is something we can pass on. Whether you are interested in programming, financial systems, operating systems or another field, think about who your work might help.

Visit the Bits to Banking website to read the lessons, try the exercises and explore the original Umicom repositories. When you learn something useful, share it with someone else.
