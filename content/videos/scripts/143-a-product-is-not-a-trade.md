# Episode 143 — A product is not a trade

*Bits to Banking · Treasury From Zero · Approximate duration: 8 minutes*

## Begin with a familiar comparison (0:00–2:00)

**Narration:** “Imagine a library has one book title and three people each borrow a copy. The title describes the book. Each borrowing record describes a separate event. Financial systems make a similar distinction between *products* and *trades*.”

**On screen:** One bond product box and two separate trade records: 100 units on Monday, 200 units on Wednesday.

## Meet the financial objects (2:00–5:30)

**Narration:** “A product describes the financial instrument—a bond, a deposit or a currency forward. A trade records a specific agreement or transaction. It needs an identity, parties, dates and terms. Two trades can involve the same bond product without being the same transaction.”

“A *legal entity* tells us who is legally involved. A *counterparty* is the other party. A *trading book* tells the bank where it records that business activity. The book is a reporting and ownership concept; it is not a replacement for the trade details.”

**On screen:** Draw four linked boxes labelled Product, Trade, Book, and Parties. Then show the supplied Calypso presentation's product/trade/book/entity concept (pages 9–15), with attribution retained.

## Test the idea (5:30–8:00)

**Narration:** “A bank buys one hundred units of a bond today and another two hundred tomorrow. How many trades have we described? Two. Could they share the same product? Yes. Why should they have different trade IDs? Because the prices, amounts and dates may differ, and any amendment must affect the correct transaction.”

“Next, we'll watch a trade move from agreement to actual settlement. That is where the difference between a product and a trade becomes even more important.”
