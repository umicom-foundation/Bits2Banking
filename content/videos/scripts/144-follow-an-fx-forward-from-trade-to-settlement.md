# Episode 144 — Following an FX forward from agreement to settlement

*Bits to Banking · Treasury From Zero · Approximate duration: 10 minutes*

## The business need (0:00–2:00)

**Narration:** “Our fictional company needs to pay a US supplier one hundred and twenty-five thousand dollars in three months. It agrees to buy those dollars from a bank, paying one hundred thousand pounds on the future date. The invented agreed rate is 1.25 dollars per pound.”

**On screen:** Company receives `USD +125,000`, pays `GBP −100,000`. Labels show the company's perspective.

## Agree, check and approve (2:00–5:00)

**Narration:** “The front office records what the parties agreed. A separate control checks the counterparty, currency direction and contractual date. Authorised people and system rules decide which actions can move the trade forward. A saved record is not automatically an approved trade.”

**On screen:** NEW → VERIFIED → AUTHORISED → CONFIRMED. Caption states “simplified classroom workflow—not a prescribed Calypso configuration”.

## From trade to real cash (5:00–8:00)

**Narration:** “As the settlement date approaches, the back office needs correct bank instructions. A processing system prepares the obligations: pay pounds and receive dollars. It may create messages for confirmation and payment, but generating a message is not the same thing as money moving. We still need settlement evidence.”

“Now suppose the pounds have left the company's account, but the dollars have not arrived. That is an investigation, not a successful settlement. The team checks the transfer and message identities, settlement instructions and bank records before deciding what action is safe.”

## Ask the viewer (8:00–10:00)

**Narration:** “If a computer log says ‘payment message sent’, does that prove the trade is settled? No. It proves something was sent. The actual obligation still needs confirmation and reconciliation. Write down the two currency legs and name one piece of evidence you would check for each.”

“Next time we will see why Calypso uses separate data services, events and processing engines to manage all these responsibilities.”
