# Episode 142 — Accounts, currencies and your first cash ladder

*Bits to Banking · Treasury From Zero · Approximate duration: 9 minutes*

## Opening (0:00–1:30)

**On screen:** Two accounts: GBP £1,000 and USD $500.

**Narration:** “Somebody says, ‘We have fifteen hundred in the bank.’ But our two accounts contain one thousand pounds and five hundred US dollars. Is fifteen hundred the correct total? No. The amounts use different currencies.”

## Explain currency and rate direction (1:30–4:00)

**Narration:** “Suppose the teaching exchange rate is one pound to one dollar and twenty-five cents. In other words, one GBP equals 1.25 USD. To express five hundred dollars in pounds at this rate, we divide five hundred by 1.25. That gives four hundred pounds. Combined with our thousand-pound account, the illustrative total is fourteen hundred pounds.”

“Real foreign-exchange transactions have spreads, fees and settlement rules. This rate is invented so that the arithmetic is easy to practise.”

## A balance on the right date (4:00–6:30)

**On screen:** Calendar Monday–Friday. Payment of £300 on Thursday; donation expected Tuesday but actually received Friday.

**Narration:** “Dates matter just as much as currencies. An approved payment for Thursday is not a bank debit on Monday. A donation expected Tuesday is not the same as a receipt confirmed by the bank. A cash ladder lists when money is expected to arrive and leave; as evidence arrives, the treasurer updates the picture.”

## Viewer exercise (6:30–9:00)

**Narration:** “Try this on paper. We start with £600. We expect £200 on Wednesday. We need to pay £500 on Tuesday. What is Tuesday's forecast balance? Now imagine the Wednesday receipt is delayed. Does that change Tuesday's problem?”

**Pause, then answer:** “Tuesday ends with £100, assuming no other movements. The later receipt cannot change that earlier figure. This is why treasury records payments by date, not just as a single monthly sum.”
