What you will learn

Explain explain raft leader elections, logs and commit majorities with explicit assumptions, an independently checkable worked example and the limits of the evidence.

Concept and mechanism

Raft is primarily crash fault tolerant and coordinates a leader-managed replicated log. Nodes use terms, votes, log comparison and majority acknowledgement to maintain committed order. A hash chain might authenticate historical bytes, but it does not implement Raft elections, heartbeats or commit rules. A majority is not just a count of equal hash strings.

Worked example

Five voting replicas need three votes for a strict majority. If only two can communicate, they cannot form a majority-backed leadership decision; a three-node connected group can, subject to log rules.

Try it yourself

Create a five-voter failure table for zero through three unreachable voters.

Reveal the fully worked answer

With zero, one or two unavailable nodes, at least three remain; with three unavailable, only two remain and majority availability is lost.

Where the proof stops

Raft does not by itself provide Byzantine fault tolerance or legal settlement finality.

A native test can verify byte-level membership but cannot by itself authenticate a participant, authorise financial transactions, establish consensus or prove neural model improvement. Record the independent root, protocol and unperformed tests.

Native implementation boundary

Use the existing Umicom Framework C23 SHA-256 and ordered record chain. The opt-in Merkle service consumes validated digests and exposes bounded membership checks. Existing Python and PowerShell teaching tools remain alternatives, not the implementation.
