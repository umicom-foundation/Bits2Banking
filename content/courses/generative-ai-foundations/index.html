Generative AI: Image and Video Training Foundations

Generative AI: Image and Video Training Foundations

Learn pixels, tensors, captions, denoising objectives, video timing, memory budgets and evaluation before planning a model-specific training run.

The conceptual exercises need paper or a calculator and original sample descriptions. Actual model training requires a separately selected compatible trainer and hardware; this course does not claim a native production diffusion trainer.

  1. Start with pixels, frames and generative models
  2. Prepare image-caption pairs with provenance
  3. Resize and batch images without silently changing the task
  4. Understand a denoising objective before training
  5. Understand latents, conditioning and image adapters
  6. Plan and evaluate a small image-training experiment
  7. Prepare video clips with timing and group identity
  8. Evaluate motion and temporal coherence
  9. Bound memory and recover image/video training work
  10. Build a native generative-AI project in verifiable stages
  11. Measure normal noise before using it in a training loop
  12. Distinguish step noise from cumulative signal
  13. Combine compatible noise predictions and measure guidance
  14. Follow a deterministic denoising trajectory
  15. Convert diffusion velocity predictions before using them
  16. Exclude padding from a training objective
  17. Weight a training objective deliberately
  18. Own the noise in a stochastic denoising step
  19. Read pixels as an explicit tensor
  20. Prepare a video sample with explicit frame order
  21. Borrow an image crop with explicit lifetime
  22. Transform pixels without changing their labels by accident
  23. Count image values before preparing training inputs
  24. Resize pixels with an explicit sampling rule
  25. Place an image on a canvas without confusing padding and masks
  26. Select video frames by time with an explicit policy
  27. Measure channel means without choosing normalization
  28. Apply explicit channel normalization
  29. Turn model values into explicit pixels
  30. Validate every frame before publishing a clip
  31. Choose an image interpolation policy

Study one step at a time

Read the explanation, predict the result, perform the exercise in a separate practice folder, then compare the result and recover from errors before continuing. A compiler check, a mathematical example and a full product qualification are different kinds of evidence.
