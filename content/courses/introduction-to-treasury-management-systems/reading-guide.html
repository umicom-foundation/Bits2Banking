Treasury Management, TMS and Calypso — from the beginning

Welcome to a course about how organisations manage money, how banks process financial contracts and how software helps them do it reliably. No previous knowledge of finance or programming is needed. You can begin with a notebook and a calculator.

We follow two connected stories. A community kitchen needs to pay its bills on time; later, a fictional company enters a foreign-exchange contract. Each chapter introduces a few new terms, demonstrates what they mean with real arithmetic, and gives you an activity that can be completed without a live bank or licensed Calypso installation.

A chapter normally takes 15–30 minutes to read and practise. Read the story first. Then do the worked numbers on paper. Look at the suggested answers after making your own attempt. If you have never used PowerShell, you can still complete every Stage 1 financial exercise.

Stage 2: follow the money through real decisions

Stage 1 introduced the main ideas. Stage 2 gives you more practice with dates, cashflows, funding and the actual processing that follows a trade. You can still complete everything with a notebook and calculator.

  1. Can we pay Friday's bills? Build a seven-day cash forecast
  2. When money earns interest: deposits, loans and the calendar
  3. Spot the funding gap before it becomes a crisis
  4. Two currencies, two dates: understand an FX swap
  5. Borrowing against securities: repos and collateral
  6. Follow one trade from booking to settlement

Try the Stage 2 practice workbook after the lessons. For a spoken explanation, watch or read Can the business pay Thursday's bill? and Why a saved trade is not a settled trade.

Useful extras

The Stage 1 glossary explains common terms. The practice workbook brings the first stage together as a set of short activities. The source guide explains how this independent course relates to the original Umicom training manuals and licensed Calypso software.

For a beginner, the important question is not “Which screen do I click?” but “What business obligation exists, what should happen next, and what proves it happened?” Screens, programming languages and product-specific APIs become much easier once that story makes sense.
