Treasury Stage 1 — learner workbook

This workbook uses fictional amounts throughout. Use a calculator if you wish, but write down the currency and the date beside each result. Each activity should be explainable to somebody who has never heard of a Treasury Management System.

Activity A — a week's cash

A local association begins Monday with £4,000. It owes £4,500 to a supplier on Tuesday, expects £1,000 on Thursday and must pay £300 in utilities on Friday.

Draw four rows: opening, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday. For each day show money in, money out and the forecast balance. Then calculate Friday's balance if the Thursday receipt is delayed by one week.

Check: Tuesday −£500; Thursday £500; Friday £200. With the Thursday receipt delayed, Friday −£800. The negative figures describe a projected shortfall, not authorisation to overdraw an account.

Activity B — name the parts of a trade

A bank and a company agree an FX forward. The bank records the transaction in LONDON_FX. The company buys USD 125,000, paying GBP 100,000 on an agreed future date.

Identify the product, trade, book and legal entities. Then show the two currency obligations from the company's perspective. Change the perspective to the bank's side and reverse the signs.

Check: Product: FX forward. Trade: this particular agreement and its identity. Book: LONDON_FX. Legal entities: bank and company. Company receives USD and pays GBP; the bank has the opposite obligations.

Activity C — interest with an explicit convention

A fictional deposit has principal £8,000, a simple annual rate of 3%, and a 60-day term on Actual/365. Calculate the interest and maturity receipt, rounding interest to pennies.

Check: £8,000 × 0.03 × 60 ÷ 365 = £39.452…; rounded interest £39.45; expected maturity £8,039.45.

Activity D — prove a payment completed

A payment system reports that an instruction has been sent. The beneficiary says they have not received the funds. Write four questions you would ask before retrying it. State which identifier, status and evidence you would preserve.

Strong answers check the instruction, settlement route, message acknowledgement and appropriate bank/account evidence. They do not resend blindly or mark the transaction settled based only on a “sent” message.

Final activity — explain the whole journey

Without using the glossary, tell a friend the difference between a product, trade, transfer, message and posting. Then open the Calypso Training Presentation to pages 9–18 and see how the original diagrams connect these objects. If a part is unclear, return to Chapters 6, 9 and 11 and try again.

You are ready for the next stage when you can explain the direction and amount of a simple cashflow, distinguish expected from settled cash and identify the evidence needed to close a payment investigation.
