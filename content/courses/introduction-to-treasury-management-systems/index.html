Introduction to Treasury Management Systems

Understand cash, commitments and the evidence a treasury system must keep.

No finance or programming knowledge is assumed. Begin with addition, subtraction and the meaning of a balance.

Follow the existing treasury course

Start with the main treasury lesson sequence. It contains the detailed explanations, worked examples, glossary and practice workbooks. Follow it in order; this page connects those lessons instead of starting a second competing sequence.

1. Understand the purpose of treasury

Read lessons 1–4: money and banks, the treasury department, why it needs a system, and accounts, balances, currencies and dates. At the end, explain the difference between the money available today and a payment expected later.

2. Follow a financial agreement

Read lessons 5–8: front, middle and back office; products, trades, books and legal entities; deposits and loans; and foreign exchange. Keep a small vocabulary list. A trade records an agreement; it is not the movement of money itself.

3. Follow the work after agreement

Read lessons 9–12: lifecycle and approvals, market data and valuation, payment messages and accounting, then the guided TMS practice trade. Complete the Stage 1 workbook. Explain what evidence is needed before calling a payment settled.

4. Practise decisions with dates and cashflows

Read lessons 13–18: a seven-day cash forecast, dated interest, a funding gap, an FX swap, collateral, and a complete booking-to-settlement case. Complete the Stage 2 workbook. These are paper exercises; no bank account, live market connection or commercial software licence is required.
