Research-grounded native computing, verifiable evidence and distributed-systems boundaries.

What you will learn

Explain preserve data sovereignty in ledger evidence with explicit assumptions, an independently checkable worked example and the limits of the evidence.

Concept and mechanism

Federated training keeps source data at its originating institution by design, but gradients and model deltas may still leak information. A ledger should ordinarily store only carefully reviewed commitments and outcome metadata, not the raw private training data or large tensor arrays. Source-specific rules may require deletion, withdrawal and privacy assessment even when hashes are used.

Worked example

An authorised school and bank each train a local model component. Only non-sensitive batch evidence is shared to a controlled audit service; neither institution discloses its raw pupil records or customer transactions.

Try it yourself

Write separate inventories for public metadata, permissioned evidence and locally restricted training data.

Reveal the fully worked answer

Data classes must be governed by rights and access policies independent of Merkle proofs.

Where the proof stops

A hash of a predictable private identifier may itself disclose information through guessing.

A native test can verify byte-level membership but cannot by itself authenticate a participant, authorise financial transactions, establish consensus or prove neural model improvement. Record the independent root, protocol and unperformed tests.

Native implementation boundary

Use the existing Umicom Framework C23 SHA-256 and ordered record chain. The opt-in Merkle service consumes validated digests and exposes bounded membership checks. Existing Python and PowerShell teaching tools remain alternatives, not the implementation.
