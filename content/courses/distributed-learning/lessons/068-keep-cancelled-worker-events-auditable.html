Research-grounded native computing, verifiable evidence and distributed-systems boundaries.

What you will learn

Explain keep cancellation events in the audit without granting rewards with explicit assumptions, an independently checkable worked example and the limits of the evidence.

Concept and mechanism

A cancelled federated assignment may need a record for operational accountability. However, it must not be counted as a completed numerical contribution or rewarded for useful learning. Separate event kinds and acceptance criteria in the ledger schema. The FederatedJob process-local ticket cannot be reconstructed from a public Merkle membership proof.

Worked example

Worker C receives round 14 assignment and cancels before producing any update. Its cancellation receipt belongs in a decision history; its accepted sample count remains zero.

Try it yourself

Describe ledger states for admitted, cancelled, completed, verified and rejected work.

Reveal the fully worked answer

Only completed and validated work contributes a delta; cancellation remains an operational event.

Where the proof stops

A ledger entry does not prove CPU/GPU-seconds were spent honestly.

A native test can verify byte-level membership but cannot by itself authenticate a participant, authorise financial transactions, establish consensus or prove neural model improvement. Record the independent root, protocol and unperformed tests.

Native implementation boundary

Use the existing Umicom Framework C23 SHA-256 and ordered record chain. The opt-in Merkle service consumes validated digests and exposes bounded membership checks. Existing Python and PowerShell teaching tools remain alternatives, not the implementation.
