Introduction to Distributed Learning, Blockchain and Verifiable AI

Sammy Hegab · Umicom Foundation · Original educational material

A new self-contained 12-lesson introductory route explaining distributed model updates and the difference between useful computation, authenticated provenance, independent evaluation and ledger consensus.

Course sequence

  • 001 — What a distributed training worker actually computes
  • 002 — Understand gradients and immutable checkpoints
  • 003 — Average three workers by hand
  • 004 — Understand model merging and non-IID data
  • 005 — LoRA and the factor-averaging trap
  • 006 — Network latency, bandwidth and compressed updates
  • 007 — Design a bounded native C23 learning job
  • 008 — Experiment with adversarial workers and receipts
  • 009 — Hash chains, signed audit logs and blockchain consensus
  • 010 — Proof of useful learning and verification economics
  • 011 — Design a LAN/WAN learning experiment
  • 012 — Research capstone: a verifiable federation proposal

Study approach

Begin with the concept and worked example, run the native C23 tests or learner laboratory, deliberately trigger a rejection, and record what has not been established. A hash is not a quality score; a numerical average is not a trained model; a blockchain is not itself distributed gradient descent.
