LESSON 86 · NATIVE C23 · AUTODIFF AND TEACHING

Learn the Mathematics Behind Rotary Training Tests

Understand every formula by reading it aloud, substituting numbers and checking its independent result.

Learning objectives

Read the formulas used by real rotary-tape tests, calculate a small loss by hand, check a gradient against independent numerical evidence, and use the linked mathematics course whenever an unfamiliar idea interrupts progress.

Mathematics help: Open the new nine-unit Maths for AI course. It starts before algebra, uses illustrated examples, and lets you reveal solutions instead of guessing. The links in this lesson take you directly to the relevant unit.

1. Every formula should answer a practical question

In engineering, a mathematical expression is a compact specification. Before writing code, ask four questions: What does each symbol represent? What units or shapes do the inputs have? What numerical example can I calculate independently? What evidence shows the software agrees with that example?

2. Calculate a loss without algebra

Suppose a teaching model produces the two numerical outputs [0.6, 0.2], and we ask it to approach the targets [0.4, 0.5]. The differences are 0.6 − 0.4 = 0.2 and 0.2 − 0.5 = −0.3. Squaring removes the sign and emphasises larger errors: 0.2² = 0.04, while (−0.3)² = 0.09.

Formula: MSE = (1/n) × Σᵢ (predictionᵢ − targetᵢ)².
Read it as: for every one of n numbers, subtract its target, square the result, add all squared differences, then divide by n. Σ means “add all the terms”; i identifies one entry; n is the total count.

There are two numbers, so n = 2. Average squared error = (0.04 + 0.09) ÷ 2 = 0.065. This is mean squared error (MSE). It is a transparent mathematical teaching loss; a language-model decoder normally uses cross-entropy for next-token prediction.

Mathematics help: Need a gentle introduction to squares, fractions or summation? Open numbers and ratios and the symbol dictionary.

3. From a loss to a gradient

How should we change the model's output to reduce this loss? For the two-number example, the gradient of MSE with respect to each prediction is 2 × (prediction − target) ÷ n. The gradients are therefore 2 × 0.2 ÷ 2 = 0.2, and 2 × (−0.3) ÷ 2 = −0.3.

The signs tell us which direction increases error locally. In a training tape, the output gradient goes backwards through attention, rotation, matrix multiplication and any trainable projection weights. This is the chain rule at work.

Mathematics help: The complete explanation of rates of change, gradients and how stages connect is in Maths Unit 6.

4. Independently check a derivative with two nearby values

The Framework test uses central finite differences to check its automatic gradients. This method evaluates a function once with a slightly larger input and once with a slightly smaller input, then measures the change over that interval.

Formula: approximate gradient ≈ [L(w + ε) − L(w − ε)] ÷ (2ε).
Read it as: w is one parameter or input; ε (epsilon) is a small positive test change; L is the loss function. Subtract the two nearby losses, then divide by twice the change size.

For a simple independent example take L(w) = w², w = 2, ε = 0.01. The larger input gives 2.01² = 4.0401. The smaller gives 1.99² = 3.9601. Their difference is 0.0800. Divide by 0.02 and get 4. The exact derivative of w² is 2w = 4 at w = 2. The two methods agree.

The epsilon value must be selected sensibly for floating-point arithmetic: too large is not local enough, but too small can lose accuracy through rounding. Real tests use finite values and declared tolerances rather than requiring bit-for-bit agreement.

5. What is a transposed rotary gradient?

During forward processing, RoPE turns pairs of coordinates by a position-based angle. To move a gradient back to the unrotated coordinate system, the training tape must apply the corresponding mathematical transpose. For a pure rotation, this undoes the angular change. This property is tested by comparing the tape gradient against the independently implemented UmiAiTrainingRotaryTensorBackward.

The necessary ideas are introduced in transpose and RoPE angles. You do not need to memorise a long equation to understand the data flow.

6. Build and test step by step on Windows

Set-Location 'C:\umicom\Umicom-Applications'
$env:Path = 'C:\msys64\ucrt64\bin;' + $env:Path

cmake -S '.\framework\learning\native-llm\prefix-work' `
  -B '.\build\windows-ucrt64-native-llm-prefix-debug' `
  -G Ninja -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Debug -DBUILD_TESTING=ON
if ($LASTEXITCODE -ne 0) { throw 'Configure failed' }

cmake --build '.\build\windows-ucrt64-native-llm-prefix-debug' `
  --target umicom-ai-training-rotary-tape-forward-test `
           umicom-ai-training-rotary-tape-rejections-test `
           umicom-native-llm-rotary-tape --parallel 2
if ($LASTEXITCODE -ne 0) { throw 'Build failed' }

ctest --test-dir '.\build\windows-ucrt64-native-llm-prefix-debug' `
  -R '^framework\.ai\.training\.rotary_tape_' `
  --output-on-failure --no-tests=error

These steps use PowerShell interactively to drive native CMake and C23 build tools. They do not introduce a PowerShell implementation of the neural network.

7. Check the evidence before reporting success

Distinguish compilation from numerical correctness and regression testing from a complete trained model. A C23 file compiling is not proof of the right gradient. Passing a tape-connected mathematical regression is not proof of a production LLM. Likewise, reduction in token-row computation is not a direct claim about milliseconds per generated token.
