Mathematical formulas, explained in plain English

A quick reference for readers who encounter an unfamiliar symbol in a native AI or LLM lesson.

No formula here is a memory test. Read the ordinary-language description, substitute small values, then open the linked lesson for a complete explanation.

1. The dot product

a · b = Σᵢ aᵢbᵢTwo equally long lists a and b contain numerical components. The centred dot means multiply matching positions and add all the products. Σ (capital sigma) is the instruction to add the indicated terms.

[2,3]·[4,5] = (2×4)+(3×5)=8+15=23. Practise with Unit 3.

2. The softmax weights

pᵢ = exp(sᵢ) / Σⱼ exp(sⱼ)s is a score. exp raises Euler's number to the score; all results are positive. Divide one value by the sum of all values so the resulting weights add to one. The i and j indices simply identify positions.

Two equal scores produce two equal weights: 0.5 and 0.5. Unit 5; Unit 13.

3. Mean squared error

MSE = Σ(prediction−target)² / NFor every output, subtract the desired target from the prediction, square the difference, add every square and divide by the number N of values. A square is the number multiplied by itself.

Predictions [2,4], targets [3,2] give ((2−3)²+(4−2)²)/2=(1+4)/2=2.5. Unit 5.

4. Rotate two numbers

x′=x cosθ − y sinθ; y′=x sinθ + y cosθx and y are a pair of coordinates. θ is an angle in radians. cos and sin are cosine and sine. The primes x′ and y′ mean the coordinates after rotation.

At θ=0, cos0=1 and sin0=0, so [2,3] stays [2,3]. Unit 7.

5. Update a weight

w(new)=w(old)−α×gw is a trainable parameter, α (alpha) is a positive learning rate and g is the gradient telling us how loss changes with that weight. Subtract a positive fraction of the gradient.

Old=2, α=0.1 and g=−2 gives 2−0.1×(−2)=2.2. Unit 10.

6. Check a gradient independently

g≈(L(w+ε)−L(w−ε))/(2ε)L is a loss function, w is a weight and ε is a small positive test change. Evaluate the loss at two nearby weights, subtract the results and divide by the difference between the test weights.

If L(w)=(w−3)², w=2, ε=0.1, then the estimate is (0.81−1.21)/0.2=−2. Unit 14.

7. Allow reasonable numerical error

|actual−reference|≤absTol+relTol×|reference|The vertical bars mean the absolute (nonnegative) size of a difference. ≤ means less than or equal to. absTol is a fixed allowance and relTol is proportional to the reference magnitude.

For reference 0.5 and actual 0.5002, difference 0.0002. With absTol 0.0001 and relTol 0.001, allowance 0.0001+0.001×0.5=0.0006, so the comparison passes. Unit 15.
