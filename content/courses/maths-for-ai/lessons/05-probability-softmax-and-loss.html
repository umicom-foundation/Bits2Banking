Probability, softmax and learning loss

Start with a plain-English idea, then a labelled formula, a worked example, and a solution you can reveal.

No mathematics qualification required. Read at your own pace. A symbol is explained the first time it appears; use the symbol guide whenever needed.

Probability is a proportion, not certainty

A probability between 0 and 1 describes a model's assigned relative likelihood for an outcome. Zero corresponds to 0%; one corresponds to 100%. In a simple example, a fair coin has probability 1/2 = 0.5 for heads. An LLM's next-token probabilities are learned numerical predictions, not guaranteed truths.

Logits: scores before probabilities

A model first computes arbitrary real-number scores, called logits. Two example scores might be 1 and 0. They do not yet sum to 1. Softmax transforms them into nonnegative probabilities that do sum to 1.

FormulaP(i) = e^(sᵢ) ÷ Σⱼ e^(sⱼ)Read P(i) as probability of item i; sᵢ as its score; e^x as the exponential of x; Σⱼ as “add this quantity for every candidate j”.

Step 1: take exponentials. e¹ ≈ 2.718 and e⁰ = 1. Step 2: add them, 2.718 + 1 = 3.718. Step 3: divide each by the total. The probabilities are approximately 0.731 and 0.269. They total 1, allowing for rounding.

Cross-entropy loss: measuring a wrong prediction

FormulaL = −ln(P(correct token))The letter L is loss. ln means the natural logarithm, which undoes the exponential. The minus sign converts ln of a probability less than 1 into a nonnegative number.

If the correct token has predicted probability 0.731, then L ≈ −ln(0.731) ≈ 0.313. A high correct-token probability gives a smaller loss. If the correct-token probability falls to 0.1, loss rises to about 2.303. Training tries to reduce the average loss across suitable examples; low training loss alone does not prove generalisation or safety.

Practice

Two logits are both zero. What probability will softmax give to each?

Show the worked solution

e⁰ = 1 for each. The total is 1 + 1 = 2, so both receive 1 ÷ 2 = 0.5 (50%).
