FOUNDATION MATHEMATICS · UNIT 01 OF 09

Numbers, fractions and percentages

Start with a plain-English idea, then a labelled formula, a worked example, and a solution you can reveal.

No mathematics qualification required. Read at your own pace. A symbol is explained the first time it appears; use the symbol guide whenever needed.

Why this comes first

Artificial intelligence software processes quantities: tokens, positions, probabilities, vectors and measurements. Before reading advanced notation, we need confidence with a small set of everyday operations. This lesson starts with money and counted work rather than assuming that you remember school mathematics.

Fractions: one part of a whole

Imagine Umicom Desk shows five support requests. Two are awaiting an answer. The fraction awaiting an answer is 2/5, read as “two out of five”. The bottom number (denominator) is the total group. The top number (numerator) is the part we are describing.

Formulafraction = selected amount ÷ total amountRead it as: divide the part by the complete group.

We substitute the numbers: 2 ÷ 5 = 0.4. A decimal is simply another way to express the same relationship.

Percentages: compare with one hundred

Formulapercentage = (part ÷ whole) × 100%The percent sign means “per hundred”.

For the two open requests: (2 ÷ 5) × 100% = 40%. For the native LLM example, full-context processing performs 96 token rows; the incremental path avoids 40 of them. Use the original 96 as the whole: (40 ÷ 96) × 100% ≈ 41.7%. It would be misleading to divide by the 56 rows left.

Ratios, units and rounding

A ratio compares two quantities, such as 2:3 for two training examples and three validation examples. Units explain what was counted. “56 rows” describes work, not 56 seconds. A reduction in arithmetic does not automatically establish an equal reduction in elapsed time because memory, caching, synchronisation and CPU behaviour matter.

The approximation sign ≈ means “close to”, not “exactly equal”. For example, 40 ÷ 96 is exactly 5/12, while 41.7% is a rounded percentage.

Try it, then check

An Umicom Bank test reviews 60 fictional transactions and flags 9 for investigation. What percentage are flagged?

Show the worked solution

Part = 9 and whole = 60. Divide 9 by 60 to get 0.15, then multiply by 100 to get 15%. This is a proportion in a teaching example, not a real bank risk statistic.
