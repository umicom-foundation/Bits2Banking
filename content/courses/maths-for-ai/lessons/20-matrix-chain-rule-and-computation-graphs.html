A journey through two calculations

Suppose a banking risk score is calculated by doubling an input and then squaring the result. A mathematical model can have the same structure: the output from one operation becomes the input of another.

u = 2w;   L = u²

w is the original number (a weight); u is an intermediate result; L is the final loss; the superscript ² means multiply a number by itself.

At w=3, u=2×3=6 and L=6×6=36.

Chain rule without mystery

dL/dw = (dL/du) × (du/dw)

The expression dL/du asks how fast loss changes with u; it equals 2u. The expression du/dw asks how fast u changes with w; it equals 2. Multiply: at u=6, gradient dL/dw = 12×2 = 24. Small changes in w therefore cause about 24 times that change in L near w=3.

Why does this matter for a tensor?

A tensor holds numbers in a structured array. A tensor operation transforms one or more arrays. During backpropagation, the Framework follows the graph of those operations in reverse order and accumulates all the different routes by which a weight influences the loss.

Practice

Repeat with w=2. What are u, L and dL/dw?

Answer

u=4, L=16 and gradient = (2×4)×2 = 16.
