Start with a familiar example

Two accountants independently calculate a report total. They may differ by a few pence due to rounding. The question is how much difference we permit before checking and why.

Absolute difference

absolute error = |measured − reference|

Take measured = 10.004 and reference = 10.000. Subtract: 10.004 − 10.000 = 0.004. The vertical bars mean take the positive size of that difference, so the error is 0.004.

Absolute versus relative tolerances

An absolute tolerance is a fixed acceptable difference. A relative tolerance scales with the reference's size. We should not divide by a reference that might be zero without a defined policy.

pass when |a − b| ≤ atol + rtol × |b|

a is the measured value; b is the reference; atol means absolute tolerance; rtol means relative tolerance; × means multiply; ≤ means less than or equal to. If a=10.004, b=10, atol=0.001, rtol=0.001, the limit is 0.001+0.001×10=0.011. Error 0.004 is within 0.011.

Practice

If the tolerance is 0.0008, is an error of 0.0012 within the bound?

Answer

No. 0.0012 is greater than 0.0008. Investigate instead of increasing the tolerance just to turn the test green.
