Angles, sine, cosine and rotary position

Start with a plain-English idea, then a labelled formula, a worked example, and a solution you can reveal.

No mathematics qualification required. Read at your own pace. A symbol is explained the first time it appears; use the symbol guide whenever needed.

An angle tells us how far to turn

Imagine a compass arrow pointing right. Rotate it a quarter turn counter-clockwise and it points up. A full turn is 360 degrees (360°); a quarter turn is 90°. Trigonometry describes those turns numerically, which is useful for rotary positional embeddings.

Degrees and radians

Mathematical software commonly measures angles in radians. A full circle is 2π radians, where π ≈ 3.14159. Thus 180° is π radians and 90° is π/2 radians. The C sin and cos functions expect radians, not degrees.

Sine and cosine in one simple rotation

Formulax′ = x cosθ − y sinθ
y′ = x sinθ + y cosθθ (theta) means angle. (x,y) are coordinates before the rotation; (x′,y′) are coordinates afterwards. sin and cos describe the horizontal and vertical parts of the turn.

For a 90° turn, cos(90°) = 0 and sin(90°) = 1. Starting with (2,0): x′ = 2×0 − 0×1 = 0; y′ = 2×1 + 0×0 = 2. The result is (0,2). It is the same length but points in a new direction.

Why an LLM rotates query and key coordinates

RoPE is short for rotary positional encoding. A transformer can rotate pairs of query/key coordinates by an angle associated with token position. Two tokens at different positions receive different rotations, which changes their attention dot product in a controlled way.

FormulaR(θ) = [[cosθ, −sinθ], [sinθ, cosθ]]R names a 2 × 2 rotation matrix. Multiplying it by the vector [x,y] reproduces the two coordinate formulas above.

In a two-dimensional example, multiplying R(90°) by [2,0] yields [0,2]. For the backward pass, the transpose Rᵀ reverses the direction of a rotation for the gradient. This is a consequence of the rotation matrix being orthogonal.

RoPE does not automatically make cached multi-layer decoding identical after old tokens are removed. Hidden states in later layers can depend on earlier context. Those details are studied in the LLM course after this mathematical introduction.

Practice

Apply a 90° counter-clockwise rotation to the vector [0,3].

Show the worked solution

x′ = 0×0 − 3×1 = −3. y′ = 0×1 + 3×0 = 0. The result is [−3,0].
