Derivatives, gradients and the chain rule

Start with a plain-English idea, then a labelled formula, a worked example, and a solution you can reveal.

No mathematics qualification required. Read at your own pace. A symbol is explained the first time it appears; use the symbol guide whenever needed.

A derivative describes local change

If you change a model parameter slightly, how much does the loss change? A derivative is a local rate of change. A gradient collects those rates for several input values or parameters. Backpropagation calculates gradients efficiently through a sequence of operations.

Start with a square

Formulaf(x) = x²   ⇒   f′(x) = 2xThe symbol f′(x) (read “f prime of x”) gives the rate of change of f for a tiny change in x.

At x = 3, f(3) = 9 and f′(3) = 6. A tiny increase of 0.01 gives an approximate output increase of 6 × 0.01 = 0.06. Calculate directly: 3.01² = 9.0601, very close to that estimate. Derivatives describe the tangent at one point, not necessarily the effect of a large change.

Chain rule: follow the steps backwards

Formulaz = 2x,   y = z²   ⇒   dy/dx = (dy/dz) × (dz/dx)The notation dy/dx means “how fast y changes when x changes”; the shared z links the two stages.

Because y = z², we have dy/dz = 2z. Because z = 2x, we have dz/dx = 2. At x = 3, z = 6. Multiply the two stage derivatives: 2 × 6 × 2 = 24. This agrees with expanding y = (2x)² = 4x² and differentiating to get 8x = 24 at x = 3.

Using a gradient to update a weight

Formulanew weight = old weight − (learning rate × gradient)The learning rate controls how large a correction to attempt; a gradient points towards increasing loss.

If an old weight is 0.8, the gradient is +0.2 and the learning rate is 0.1, then 0.8 − (0.1 × 0.2) = 0.78. This one step is not a guarantee of a lower loss; training checks actual outcomes and valid numerical limits.

Practice

For y = (3x)², what is dy/dx at x = 2?

Show the worked solution

Let z = 3x. Then dy/dz = 2z and dz/dx = 3. At x = 2, z = 6, so dy/dx = 2 × 6 × 3 = 36. Equivalently y = 9x², so derivative = 18x = 36 at x = 2.
