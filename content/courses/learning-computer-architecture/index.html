Introduction to Computers

A first course for anyone curious about computers. No coding, specialist vocabulary or previous technical study is expected.

Use paper, conversation or a device you have permission to use. No installation is needed to begin. The first lessons explain how to follow a page as well as the computing ideas. There are no timed exercises, accounts or automatic progress tracking.

Choose how to take part

Read independently, listen with a reading tool, or work with a friend, parent, carer or teacher. You may explain answers aloud, write them or draw them. Browser zoom and printing can help; the written activities do not require seeing colours.

Lessons in order

  1. Start with curiosity, not experience — Find your way through a lesson and explain one thing you have learned.
  2. What does a computer do? — Separate information going in, instructions being followed, and a result coming out.
  3. Keep something you can find again — Explain a file, a folder and a path, and distinguish editing from saving.
  4. What the computer keeps while it works — Distinguish working memory from storage and explain a bit and a byte.
  5. Give instructions and check what happens — Use order, a choice and repetition to describe a task, then test an expectation.
  6. Read a command before running it — Recognise a command name, an argument, a prompt and a working folder.
  7. How applications, an OS and a kernel fit together — Explain the role of each layer and distinguish Umicom OS from Umicom Kernel.
  8. Choose your next learning route — Choose an achievable next step and recognise which knowledge a practical lab expects.

Keep your place

Write the lesson title and your next action on paper, or save a bookmark. Nothing is uploaded and the pages do not save completion for you.
