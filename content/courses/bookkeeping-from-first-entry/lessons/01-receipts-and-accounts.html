Trace a receipt into accounts

Course contents Previous course — Mathematics for Application Builders Next lesson — Record both sides of a transaction

What you will learn

Distinguish a source document, a transaction and an account, then check the accounting equation.

Sort the evidence

A source document is a record such as a dated receipt. A transaction is the event the receipt describes. An account is a named place to collect related amounts. For this workshop, Cash describes money held and Equipment describes things such as a label maker.

The accounting equation is Assets = Liabilities + Equity. Assets are resources the organisation controls. Liabilities are obligations it owes to others. Equity is the remaining interest after subtracting liabilities. Always identify whose records you are writing.

Work it through

  1. An owner brings $100 into the community workshop. Cash is now 10000 cents, liabilities are zero, and owner capital in equity is 10000 cents.
  2. The workshop pays $25 for equipment. Enter the cost as 2500 cents and keep the receipt reference E-01. Cash falls to 7500 cents. Equipment rises to 2500 cents. Assets still total 10000 cents and equity remains 10000 cents.
  3. Before recording each purchase, name both sides of the event. Here, one resource was exchanged for another. No new debt appeared.
  4. Preserve the original date, ID and amount. A reviewer should be able to start at the balance and follow it back to E-01.

Try it and check

From the state above, the workshop borrows $30 in cash. What are Cash, Equipment, total Assets, Liabilities and Equity?

Check: Cash = 10500 cents, Equipment = 2500 cents, Assets = 13000 cents, Liabilities = 3000 cents, Equity = 10000 cents. Record the loan as an obligation. The original investment and the borrowed money both increase cash, with different effects on the other side.

Apply it to an Umicom project

Plan an Umicom Bank practice screen that shows a synthetic reference, account and amount. Put the record’s origin beside it. A separate screen can later show the accounting totals derived from those rows.
