# Compare two easy toy targets

![Umicom Foundation](../../assets/branding/umicom-wordmark.svg){ .umicom-wordmark .umicom-wordmark-light width="215" }
![Umicom Foundation](../../assets/branding/umicom-wordmark-on-dark.svg){ .umicom-wordmark .umicom-wordmark-dark width="215" }

A paper model uses independent, uniformly distributed hashes. Target A asks for four leading zero bits, and target B asks for six.

1. Calculate how many equally likely leading bit patterns are possible for each length, using 2 raised to the bit count.
2. Write one divided by that count as the per-attempt probability, then invert it to compare the expected trials.

**Check your result.** Target A has a probability of 1/16 and an expectation of 16 trials. Target B has a probability of 1/64 and an expectation of 64 trials, four times as many.
