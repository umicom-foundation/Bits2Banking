# Faith, service and technology

The Umicom Foundation's work is shaped by a belief that knowledge should benefit people. Within the Islamic tradition, worship and good character are closely connected to honesty, keeping promises, justice in financial dealings and the care owed to other people. Technology is one way these values can be put into practice.

## Learning with a purpose

Learning how computers work can open opportunities for someone to build, repair and improve useful tools. Learning to explain a concept clearly can be just as valuable: it helps the next person develop the confidence to try.

Consider a person who learns to write a small programme for managing community donations. The programme might begin with a list of transactions. As their skills grow, they can add balanced accounting entries, permissions, approval controls and a report that explains where funds were used.

Each improvement addresses a responsibility. A correct calculation protects resources. A clear interface helps people avoid mistakes. An audit record makes a decision reviewable. A useful guide helps others maintain the system.

## Trust and accountability in software

In Islamic ethics, **amānah** is the responsibility of holding a trust. Software can support trustworthy conduct, although code alone cannot guarantee it. When designing a financial system, we should be able to explain who may authorise a payment, which records are authoritative, how an error is corrected, and how a decision can be reviewed.

Practise these ideas in [double-entry accounting](../lessons/07-practical-domains/31-accounting.md) and in the [Treasury Management Systems](../lessons/07-practical-domains/32-treasury.md) lesson. Both use fictional data, so you can focus on the controls without handling anyone's money.

## Justice and cooperation

Fairness is relevant to software design. Clear instructions make technical education accessible. Open code can be inspected and improved. Review and testing help us detect errors before they cause harm. Neither a technology label nor an attractive interface proves that a system is equitable; the people designing and operating it must remain accountable.

A small project can be valuable without being commercially large: a note-taking tool, a school resource, an accessible website, or a system that helps organise community work.
